# The Wellness Company — reference

Source: https://thewellness.company/#top · Toronto · contact@thewellness.company · © 2026

## What they sell

Software, not supplements. They build health tools that unify data from wearables, labs, habits and connected products.

| Product              | Line               | What it does                                                              |
| -------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Tempo** (flagship) | Personal health OS | Aggregates wearables, labs, habits, connected products into one dashboard |

## Ideal customer

The quantified-self adherent one tier above casual. 28–45, high income, tech-literate, treats longevity as a project. Already owns an Oura or Whoop, already pays for lab panels, already cold-plunges.

Their problem is not motivation — it is **fragmentation**. Five apps, no synthesis. They are being sold coherence, not the habit.

## What they expect on landing

Proof of taste before proof of features. Within ~4 seconds they want to know:

1. Does it connect to what I already wear?
2. Does it _tell_ me something, or just plot me?
3. Was this built by people who care as much as I do?

They are not looking for a pricing table in the hero. (The live site shows no pricing, no testimonials, no logo wall.)

## Theme: calm is the argument

The category default is red rings, streaks and urgency — anxiety as an engagement mechanic. This site deliberately inverts that. A one-word headline, five one-word pillars, heavy whitespace, no countdown. The restraint _is_ the positioning: a health OS that makes you feel less monitored, not more.

Visual: white type on dark ground, dashboard screenshots and product photography, modern health-tech minimalism.

## Copywriting rules for this voice

- **Headlines: 2–5 words, declarative.** "LiveMore." not "Take control of your health today."
- **One plain sentence carries the whole explanation.** No "revolutionary," no adjective stacks.
- **CTAs invite, never command.** "Let's begin" / "Start now" — first-person plural, low pressure. Nothing implying you're behind.
- **Nouns over benefits.** Name what things _are_ (Our belief, Our products, Our team).
- **One idea per screen.** Full-height sections with silence around them.
- **Lowercase-calm punctuation.** Italics for emphasis, not bold or caps.

### Anti-patterns (drift out of register)

Enterprise proof bars ("Trusted by 500+ global enterprises"), dual high-saturation CTAs with glow shadows, `↗` arrows, urgency verbs, streak/scarcity framing.
